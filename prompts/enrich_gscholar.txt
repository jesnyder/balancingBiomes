A DOI (Digital Object Identifier) is a permanent, standardized identifier used to uniquely identify scholarly works such as journal articles, datasets, and reports. It consists of two main parts separated by a slash: a prefix and a suffix. The prefix always begins with 10, which designates the DOI system, followed by a number that identifies the publisher or registrant (for example, 10.1016 for Elsevier or 10.1038 for Nature). The suffix is assigned by the publisher and uniquely identifies the specific item; it often encodes information such as the journal name, year of publication, and article number, but its structure is not standardized. When combined, the prefix and suffix form a globally unique identifier that can be resolved through https://doi.org/, ensuring persistent access to the referenced work even if its web location changes.

The doi can be at the end of middle of a url.
